Movies

Not everyone has two hours to spare for a movie. Some drag; some fly. But one thing I know: ratings lie. For me, films were either alive or dead, with no in-between. The ones I loved all ranked the same, even if I obsessed over some more than others. Maybe that’s hypocrisy. Maybe it was just laziness to dissect why they felt different.

Watching with someone else, though? That’s where the magic twists. You tear apart scenes like critics, even if you couldn’t do better. (Let’s be real: most of us just enjoy the act of judging.) And when it’s your favorite film? You pray they catch the same details that gutted you, with no rewinds allowed. Their reaction becomes part of the story, part of whether you love or hate the movie itself.

But how you processed movies… we could never debate that. It just seemed like you always had a positive view and an appreciation for anything we watched. I’m starting to realize that perhaps there was more I missed. Truthfully, I am never really the type of person who can fully process a story through its graphics. I get stranded with the emotions I can imitate, and that shifts my entire perception of the movie. It’s strange, because my favorite genres are horror and tragic dramas. I’m unsure what that says. Whose emotions am I actually capturing: the villain’s or the hero’s?

But one thing I knew: somehow, in all that emotional turmoil, a simple glimpse from my periphery of you enjoying the movie would bring a smile to my face. Nothing else was really needed for me to be happy.

Now, when I rewatch those movies, I find myself wondering how you processed them. Whenever I watch any, I am transported back to the same place, and I still can’t figure out what you were thinking. Maybe it was always that way. Sometimes I still wonder if the film’s rolling. If I’m stuck in the climax, waiting for a resolution that’s already passed me by--or were you watching alone?
